Understanding Your Rights: A Guide to Civil Litigation in Nepal
Legal Advocacy and Litigation

Civil litigation is the process of resolving disputes between individuals, businesses, or organisations through the court system. This guide walks you through the stages of civil litigation in Nepal—from filing a plaint to final judgment—explaining what to expect at each step and how to prepare.

Civil litigation is the legal process through which individuals, businesses, and organisations resolve disputes that do not involve criminal charges. In Nepal, civil litigation is governed by the Civil Procedure Code, 2017 and the Civil Procedure Rules, 2018. Understanding the process is essential for anyone involved in a civil dispute.

What is a Civil Dispute?

A civil dispute arises when one party believes that another party has failed to fulfil a legal obligation, causing harm or loss. Common civil disputes include:

  • Contractual disagreements (e.g., breach of contract, non-payment)
  • Property and land disputes
  • Family law matters (divorce, custody, inheritance)
  • Tort claims (negligence, defamation, personal injury)
  • Employment and labour disputes
  • Debt recovery and financial claims

Stages of Civil Litigation in Nepal

1. Pre-Litigation Stage

Before filing a lawsuit, parties are generally expected to attempt settlement through negotiation or mediation. Courts in Nepal encourage alternative dispute resolution to reduce case backlogs. If settlement fails, the plaintiff may proceed to file a claim.

2. Filing the Plaint

The plaintiff files a written statement of claim (plaint) with the appropriate court. The plaint must include:

  • Names and addresses of the parties
  • Clear statement of facts and legal grounds
  • The relief or remedy sought
  • Supporting documents and evidence

The court examines the plaint and may issue a summons to the defendant.

3. Written Statement and Defence

The defendant is required to file a written statement responding to the plaintiff's claims within the prescribed time. The written statement must address each allegation and may raise counterclaims or preliminary objections.

4. Discovery and Evidence

Both parties exchange relevant documents and information. Witnesses may be examined, and expert testimony may be presented. The court may also order inspection of property or documents.

5. Trial

The trial involves examination of witnesses, presentation of evidence, and arguments from both sides. The court hears all submissions and examines the facts and law.

6. Judgment and Decree

After the trial, the court issues a judgment and decree, which is the final decision determining the rights and liabilities of the parties. The decree is executable, meaning the winning party may enforce it through court processes.

7. Appeals

Either party may appeal to a higher court if they believe the judgment contains legal or factual errors. In Nepal, cases may be appealed from District Courts to the High Court, and subsequently to the Supreme Court on points of law.

Limitation Periods

Civil claims in Nepal are subject to limitation periods. The Limitation Act, 2018 prescribes specific timeframes within which claims must be filed. Generally:

  • Contractual claims: 6 years
  • Property disputes: 10 years
  • Tort claims: 2 years
  • Debt recovery: 3 years

Failing to file within the limitation period may bar the claim entirely.

Costs and Time

Civil litigation can be time-consuming and expensive. Costs include court fees, lawyer fees, expert fees, and other incidentals. Litigation may take months or years depending on the complexity and backlog. However, settlement and alternative dispute resolution can significantly reduce costs and time.

Key Considerations

Before initiating or defending a civil lawsuit, consider:

  • The strength of your legal position
  • The cost-benefit analysis of litigation
  • Availability of evidence and witnesses
  • Limitation periods and deadlines
  • The possibility of settlement

Criminal Defence in Nepal: What to Do When You Are Charged
Legal Advocacy and Litigation

Being charged with a criminal offence is a serious and stressful experience. This article explains the criminal justice process in Nepal, your rights as an accused person, and what steps you should take to protect your freedom and reputation.

Criminal charges can arise from various situations—from traffic violations and minor offences to serious crimes such as theft, fraud, assault, or corruption. In Nepal, the criminal justice system is governed by the Criminal Procedure Code, 2017 and the Criminal Offences Act, 2017. When you are charged, your immediate response and the quality of your legal representation can significantly impact the outcome.

Your Rights When Charged

The Constitution of Nepal guarantees fundamental rights to every accused person, including:

  • Presumption of Innocence: You are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond reasonable doubt.
  • Right to Legal Representation: You have the right to be represented by an advocate of your choice. If you cannot afford one, the state must provide legal aid.
  • Right to Silence: You are not obliged to confess or incriminate yourself.
  • Right to Information: You must be informed of the charges against you and the grounds for your arrest.
  • Right to a Fair Trial: You are entitled to a public hearing by an independent and impartial court, with the opportunity to present evidence and cross-examine witnesses.
  • Right to Bail: You have the right to apply for bail, and the court must grant bail unless there are compelling reasons to deny it.

Steps to Take When You Are Charged

1. Contact a Lawyer Immediately

Do not speak to police or any authority without legal representation. Your lawyer will advise you on what to say, what not to say, and how to protect your rights.

2. Exercise Your Right to Silence

You are not required to answer questions that may incriminate you. Politely inform the authorities that you wish to speak with your lawyer before answering questions.

3. Understand the Charges

Ask for a clear explanation of the charges against you. Your lawyer will review the allegations and advise on the strength of the case against you.

4. Preserve Evidence

Keep any documents, communications, or evidence that may support your defence. Your lawyer can guide you on what is relevant.

5. Apply for Bail

If arrested, your lawyer will apply for bail. Bail may be granted with or without conditions, depending on the nature of the offence and the risk of flight or further offending.

The Criminal Trial Process

Stage 1: Investigation

Police conduct an investigation to collect evidence. You have the right to be present during key proceedings and to have your lawyer present.

Stage 2: Filing of Charges

If the investigation reveals sufficient evidence, charges are filed in the competent court. The charges must clearly state the offence and the facts constituting it.

Stage 3: Preliminary Hearing

The court examines the charges and may hold a preliminary hearing to determine if there is sufficient evidence to proceed to trial.

Stage 4: Trial

The trial involves the presentation of evidence, examination of witnesses, and arguments from both the prosecution and the defence. The prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt.

Stage 5: Judgment and Sentencing

If convicted, the court delivers its judgment and imposes a sentence. Sentences may include imprisonment, fines, community service, or other penalties.

Stage 6: Appeal

You have the right to appeal a conviction or sentence to a higher court. Your lawyer will advise on the grounds for appeal and the likelihood of success.

Penalties and Sentencing

Penalties for criminal offences in Nepal vary depending on the nature and severity of the crime. The Criminal Offences Act, 2017 prescribes sentences ranging from fines to life imprisonment. Some offences also carry additional penalties such as confiscation of property or disqualification from holding public office.

Why Legal Representation Matters

Criminal defence requires thorough knowledge of criminal law, court procedures, and evidentiary rules. An experienced advocate can:

  • Advise you on your rights and options
  • Challenge the prosecution's evidence and arguments
  • Present a compelling defence
  • Negotiate plea bargains where appropriate
  • Represent you effectively at trial and on appeal

Navigating Regulatory Compliance: A Checklist for Nepali Businesses
Legal Consultancy and Advisory

Regulatory compliance is essential for every business in Nepal. This checklist covers key compliance areas—company registration, tax registration, labour law, and sectoral licensing—to help you stay on the right side of the law.

Running a business in Nepal requires compliance with a wide range of laws and regulations. Failure to comply can result in penalties, fines, legal disputes, and reputational damage. This guide provides a practical compliance checklist for businesses of all sizes operating in Nepal.

Corporate Registration and Structure

  • Company Registration: Every business must be registered with the Office of the Company Registrar under the Companies Act, 2006. Choose an appropriate structure: private limited company, public limited company, sole proprietorship, or partnership.
  • PAN Registration: Obtain a Permanent Account Number (PAN) from the Inland Revenue Department for tax purposes.
  • VAT Registration: If your annual turnover exceeds the prescribed threshold (currently NPR 5 million), you must register for Value Added Tax (VAT).
  • Business License: Obtain a business license from the local municipality or relevant authority based on your business activity.

Tax Compliance

  • File Annual Tax Returns: All businesses must file annual income tax returns. Tax rates vary depending on business type and income.
  • VAT Returns: VAT-registered businesses must file periodic VAT returns and pay VAT to the government.
  • Tax Deducted at Source (TDS): Businesses must deduct TDS on payments such as salaries, rent, and professional fees, and remit it to the government.
  • Maintain Proper Records: Keep accurate financial records, invoices, receipts, and accounting books for at least 5 years.

Labour Law Compliance

  • Employment Contracts: Provide written employment contracts to all employees as required under the Labour Act, 2017.
  • Minimum Wages: Ensure that employees are paid at least the government-prescribed minimum wage.
  • Social Security: Register employees with the Social Security Fund (SSF) and contribute to the fund.
  • Working Hours and Overtime: Comply with laws on working hours (8 hours per day, 48 hours per week) and overtime provisions.
  • Health and Safety: Provide a safe working environment and comply with occupational health and safety standards.
  • Termination Procedures: Follow proper procedures for termination, including notice periods, severance pay, and documentation.

Sector-Specific Licenses and Approvals

Depending on your business activity, you may require specific licenses or approvals from regulatory authorities. Examples include:

  • Financial institutions: Nepal Rastra Bank
  • Insurance companies: Insurance Board
  • Telecommunications: Nepal Telecommunications Authority
  • Educational institutions: Ministry of Education
  • Healthcare: Ministry of Health
  • Tourism and hospitality: Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Civil Aviation
  • Mining and extraction: Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies

Annual Filings and Reporting

  • Annual General Meeting (AGM): Hold an AGM within prescribed timelines.
  • Financial Statements: Prepare and file annual financial statements with the Office of the Company Registrar.
  • Audit: Have your financial statements audited by a qualified auditor if required by law.
  • Compliance Certificates: File compliance certificates with relevant authorities.

Data Protection and Privacy

With the enactment of the Individual Privacy Act, 2075 and the Information Technology Act, businesses must comply with data protection obligations. Key requirements include:

  • Obtaining consent before collecting personal data
  • Protecting personal data from unauthorised access and breaches
  • Providing individuals with access to their data
  • Data breach notification to the competent authority

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance

Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act, 2008, businesses in designated sectors (e.g., financial institutions, real estate, casinos) must establish AML policies and procedures. This includes:

  • Customer due diligence
  • Suspicious transaction reporting
  • Record keeping
  • Staff training

Common Compliance Pitfalls

  • Failing to file returns on time
  • Not updating registrations after changes
  • Non-compliance with labour laws
  • Ignoring sectoral licensing requirements
  • Poor record keeping

Understanding Contract Law in Nepal: Key Clauses Every Business Should Know
Legal Consultancy and Advisory

Contracts are the backbone of commercial transactions. This article explains the essential clauses every business contract should include, common pitfalls, and how to draft agreements that protect your interests.

Contracts are legally binding agreements that govern relationships and transactions between parties. In Nepal, contract law is primarily governed by the Contract Act, 2000 and the general principles of contract law derived from English common law. Understanding key contractual clauses is essential for any business.

Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

For a contract to be legally enforceable in Nepal, it must contain:

  • Offer and Acceptance: One party makes an offer, and the other party accepts it unequivocally.
  • Consideration: Something of value must be exchanged between the parties (money, goods, services).
  • Intention to Create Legal Relations: The parties must intend their agreement to have legal effect.
  • Capacity: Both parties must have the legal capacity to contract (age, mental capacity).
  • Legality: The purpose of the contract must be lawful.
  • Certainty: The terms of the contract must be clear and certain.

Key Clauses Every Business Contract Should Include

1. Parties Clause

Clearly identify all parties to the contract, including their full legal names, addresses, and registration numbers (if applicable).

2. Scope of Work (Performance Obligations)

Describe in detail the goods, services, or work to be provided. This clause should be specific to avoid disputes over what is expected.

3. Price and Payment Terms

Specify the total price, payment schedule, method of payment, currency, and consequences of late payment (e.g., interest, penalties).

4. Delivery and Performance Schedule

Set out when goods will be delivered or services completed. Include provisions for delays and extensions.

5. Warranties and Representations

Include assurances that the goods or services meet certain standards. Warranties may be express or implied.

6. Limitation of Liability

This clause limits the amount and scope of liability that each party can claim from the other. It may cap damages or exclude certain types of losses (e.g., consequential damages).

7. Indemnification

Requires one party to compensate the other for certain losses or damages. This is common in contracts involving third-party claims.

8. Confidentiality

Protects proprietary or sensitive information exchanged during the contract term. This is essential for business agreements involving trade secrets, customer data, or strategic plans.

9. Termination and Renewal

Specifies the conditions under which the contract can be terminated, notice periods, and whether the contract automatically renews.

10. Dispute Resolution

Choose a mechanism for resolving disputes (negotiation, mediation, arbitration, or court litigation). In Nepal, it is common to designate a specific court or arbitration body.

11. Governing Law

Specify which jurisdiction's laws will govern the contract. In Nepal, the law of Nepal will apply unless otherwise agreed.

12. Force Majeure

Excuses performance if an extraordinary event (e.g., natural disaster, war, pandemic) prevents performance. This clause has become increasingly important in recent years.

13. Amendments

Specify that changes to the contract must be in writing and signed by both parties.

Common Contract Pitfalls

  • Ambiguous Language: Vague terms lead to disputes. Use precise language.
  • Unreasonable Terms: Terms that are too one-sided may be challenged in court.
  • Failure to Address Risks: Contracts should identify and allocate risks appropriately.
  • Insufficient Due Diligence: Failing to check the other party's legal standing or financial capability.
  • Ignoring Local Law: Ensure compliance with Nepali laws and regulations.

Oral Contracts vs. Written Contracts

While oral contracts can be legally binding in Nepal, written contracts are strongly preferred. Written agreements provide clear evidence of the terms, reduce disputes, and are easier to enforce in court.

Corporate Governance Best Practices for Nepali Companies

Corporate governance refers to the system of rules, practices, and processes by which companies are directed and controlled. Effective governance ensures accountability, transparency, and fairness in a company's relationship with shareholders, management, and other stakeholders.

The Corporate Governance Framework in Nepal

In Nepal, corporate governance is governed by the Companies Act, 2006, the Securities Act, 2007 (for listed companies), and regulations issued by the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON). The Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) also imposes governance requirements on listed companies.

Key Principles of Good Corporate Governance

1. Board Composition and Independence

The board of directors should include a balance of executive and non-executive directors. Independent directors bring objective perspectives and help prevent conflicts of interest.

2. Board Committees

Establish committees to oversee specific areas such as audit, remuneration, and governance. Audit committees, in particular, play a critical role in financial oversight and compliance.

3. Transparency and Disclosure

Companies should disclose accurate and timely information about financial performance, governance practices, and material risks. This builds trust with shareholders and the public.

4. Shareholder Rights

Protecting shareholder rights is fundamental to good governance. Shareholders should be able to vote, access information, and participate in shareholder meetings.

5. Ethical Conduct and Integrity

Companies should adopt codes of conduct, anti-corruption policies, and whistleblower mechanisms to promote ethical behaviour.

6. Risk Management

Effective governance includes identifying, assessing, and managing risks. Companies should have robust internal controls and risk management frameworks.

Recent Amendments to the Companies Act, 2006

The Companies (Amendment) Act, 2025 introduced several governance-related changes:

  • Sweat Equity: Companies may now issue sweat equity to employees, enabling employee participation in the company's growth.
  • Employee Share Schemes: Provisions for issuing shares to employees have been simplified.
  • Transparency Requirements: Enhanced disclosure obligations for board composition and related-party transactions.

Best Practices for Boards and Directors

  • Hold regular board meetings and maintain proper minutes.
  • Ensure directors are qualified and fulfil their fiduciary duties.
  • Disclose director interests and related-party transactions.
  • Engage independent auditors for financial oversight.
  • Promote a speak-up culture where concerns can be raised without fear.

Why Corporate Governance Matters

  • Investor Confidence: Strong governance attracts domestic and foreign investment.
  • Legal Compliance: Reduces legal risks and penalties.
  • Reputation: Enhances the company's standing with stakeholders.
  • Long-term Sustainability: Promotes responsible decision-making and business continuity.

Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) are the backbone of Nepal's economy, contributing significantly to employment and GDP. However, SMEs face unique risks due to limited resources, capacity constraints, and regulatory complexities. Effective risk management is essential for survival and growth.

Understanding Business Risks

Business risks can be categorised into several types:

  • Legal Risks: Non-compliance, lawsuits, contract disputes, regulatory fines.
  • Financial Risks: Cash flow problems, bad debts, currency fluctuations.
  • Operational Risks: Supply chain disruptions, employee turnover, equipment failure.
  • Strategic Risks: Competition, market shifts, changing customer preferences.
  • Compliance Risks: Failure to meet regulatory requirements.
  • Reputational Risks: Negative public perception, media scrutiny.

Step 1: Identify Risks

Conduct a comprehensive risk assessment of your business. Ask yourself:

  • What could go wrong in our operations?
  • Are we complying with all applicable laws?
  • What legal liabilities do we face?
  • Do we have contracts that protect our interests?
  • What would happen if a key employee left?

Step 2: Assess and Prioritise Risks

Evaluate the likelihood and potential impact of each risk. Prioritise risks that are both likely and severe. Use a risk matrix to visualise your exposure.

Step 3: Implement Mitigation Strategies

Legal Risk Mitigation

  • Engage a legal advisor for compliance checks and contract reviews.
  • Use written contracts for all business relationships.
  • Ensure proper licensing and registration.
  • Stay updated on regulatory changes.

Financial Risk Mitigation

  • Maintain proper financial records and accounting systems.
  • Build cash reserves for emergencies.
  • Diversify income streams.
  • Insure key assets and liabilities.

Operational Risk Mitigation

  • Develop standard operating procedures.
  • Cross-train employees to cover key roles.
  • Maintain relationships with multiple suppliers.
  • Invest in security and data protection.

Compliance Risk Mitigation

  • Maintain a compliance calendar with key filing dates.
  • Regularly review tax and regulatory obligations.
  • Engage professionals for compliance support.

Step 4: Monitor and Review

Risk management is not a one-time exercise. Conduct periodic reviews, update your risk register, and adjust strategies as circumstances change. Regular monitoring helps you stay ahead of emerging risks.

Common Risk Management Mistakes

  • Ignoring risks until they materialise
  • Over-relying on informal agreements

Nepal's Evolving Legal Landscape: Key Legislative Changes in 2025–2026

The period 2025–2026 has seen a flurry of legislative activity in Nepal, reflecting the government's commitment to economic reform, regulatory modernisation, and international compliance. This article provides an overview of the most significant legal developments and their practical implications.

1. Budget 2025/26 and Tax Reforms

The budget for fiscal year 2025/26 introduced significant changes to Nepal's tax regime:

  • Reduction of Income Tax Brackets: The number of individual income tax brackets was reduced from six to five.
  • Maximum Tax Rate Reduced: The maximum tax rate for individuals was reduced from 39% to 29%.
  • Tax on Super Rich: A new 10% tax was introduced on the super-rich (individuals with annual income exceeding NPR 100 million).
  • IT Sector Incentives: Tax incentives were introduced for the Information Technology (IT) sector to promote digitalisation.
  • Tax on Club and Casino Licences: Reduced from NPR 25 million to NPR 10 million to encourage tourism.
  • VAT Changes: VAT on electricity increased from 5% to 13%.

2. Companies (Amendment) Act, 2025

The Companies Act was amended to address emerging needs in the corporate sector:

  • Sweat Equity: Companies may issue sweat equity to employees, facilitating employee participation.
  • Employee Share Schemes: Simplified provisions for issuing shares to employees.
  • Gender Representation: Listed companies are required to have at least one female director.
  • Transparency Enhancements: Enhanced disclosure obligations for board composition and related-party transactions.

3. Customs Act, 2082

The new Customs Act aims to modernise Nepal's customs procedures and align them with international standards. Key features include:

  • Simplified Procedures: Streamlined customs clearance for importers and exporters.
  • Enhanced Enforcement: Strengthened enforcement mechanisms to combat smuggling and customs fraud.
  • Digitalisation: Promotion of electronic filing and payment systems.
  • Risk-Based Inspection: A risk-based approach to customs inspections, prioritising high-risk shipments.

4. Public Procurement (Amendment) Act, 2025

Significant amendments to the Public Procurement Act include:

  • Criminal Liability for Contractors: Contractors may face criminal liability for procurement irregularities.
  • Enhanced Scrutiny: Increased scrutiny of procurement processes and documentation.
  • E-Procurement: Expanded use of electronic procurement systems.

5. Media Council Bill

The Media Council Bill proposes the establishment of a council to regulate media content and ethics. Provisions include:

  • Registration of Media Houses: Mandatory registration of all media houses.
  • Ethical Guidelines: Establishment of ethical guidelines for media content.
  • Complaint Mechanism: A mechanism for handling complaints against media outlets.

6. E-Commerce Act, 2025

With the rapid growth of e-commerce, the E-Commerce Act introduces a regulatory framework for online businesses. Key provisions include:

  • Registration: Mandatory registration of e-commerce platforms.
  • Consumer Protection: Enhanced consumer protection provisions, including refund rights and dispute resolution mechanisms.
  • Data Protection: Data privacy obligations for e-commerce platforms.
  • Tax Collection: Mechanisms for collecting taxes on e-commerce transactions.

7. Information Technology and Cybersecurity Act, 2082

This Act addresses cybersecurity challenges and regulates IT-related activities. Key features include:

  • Cybersecurity Standards: Minimum cybersecurity standards for businesses.
  • Data Breach Notification: Mandatory reporting of data breaches to the competent authority.
  • Cyber Offences: Penalties for cybercrimes, including hacking, identity theft, and online fraud.
  • Digital Governance: Promotion of digital governance and online service delivery.

8. Land Law Amendments

Recent amendments to land laws include:

  • Removal of Land Ceiling: The land ceiling for housing and residential real estate development has been removed.
  • Land Revenue Act: Amendments to the Land Revenue Act to improve land valuation and taxation.
  • Land Information System: Expansion of the land information system (भू-सूचना प्रणाली).

9. Monetary Policy 2025/26

The central bank's monetary policy introduced measures to support economic growth and financial stability:

  • Lending Rates: Reduced lending rates to promote business investment.
  • Credit Flow: Measures to ensure credit flow to productive sectors.
  • Financial Inclusion: Initiatives to enhance financial inclusion and access to banking.

10. Labour Law Amendments

Proposed amendments to labour laws aim to enhance worker protection and improve the business environment:

  • Flexible Work Arrangements: Provisions for flexible work arrangements, including remote work.
  • Skill Development: Increased emphasis on skill development and vocational training.
  • Social Security: Expansion of social security coverage to informal sector workers.

What These Changes Mean for You

  • For Businesses: New compliance obligations but also new opportunities, especially for the IT sector, e-commerce, and foreign investment.
  • For Individuals: Changes in tax obligations, but also enhanced consumer protection and data privacy.
  • For Legal Professionals: A dynamic legal environment requiring continuous learning and adaptation.

Every business operating in Nepal must comply with tax laws. Non-compliance can result in penalties, fines, and legal disputes. This guide outlines the key tax compliance obligations for Nepali businesses and provides practical advice on meeting them.

Step 1: Register for Taxes

  • PAN Registration: Obtain a Permanent Account Number (PAN) from the Inland Revenue Department. This is the unique tax identification number for your business.
  • VAT Registration: If your annual turnover exceeds NPR 5 million (or a lower threshold for certain services), you must register for VAT.

Who Must Comply?

AML obligations apply to businesses in designated sectors, including:

  • Financial institutions (banks, insurance companies, microfinance institutions)
  • Real estate and land registration offices

  • Legal professionals (advocates and law firms)

  • Transactions that may be linked to criminal activities

Estate Planning in Nepal: Wills, Succession, and Inheritance
Personal Legal Services, Documentation and Conveyancing

Estate planning ensures your assets are distributed according to your wishes. This article explains the legal framework for wills, succession, and inheritance in Nepal.

Read More →

Estate planning involves organising the distribution of your assets after your death. In Nepal, estate matters are governed by a combination of personal laws (based on religion), the Succession Act, 2018, and the General Code (Muluki Civil Code, 2018). Understanding these laws is essential for anyone planning their estate.

Key Legal Framework

Estate planning in Nepal is governed by:

  • Muluki Civil Code, 2018 (General Code): Contains detailed provisions on succession and inheritance.
  • Succession Act, 2018: Provides additional clarity on succession matters.
  • Personal Laws: Based on religion (Hindu, Buddhist, Muslim, Christian) for certain aspects of succession.

Intestate Succession vs. Testamentary Succession

Intestate Succession

Intestate succession occurs when a person dies without leaving a valid will. In such cases, the law determines who inherits the estate. Under the Muluki Civil Code, the estate is distributed among the heirs (children, spouse, parents, and other relatives) according to specific rules. The order of succession is:

  • First: Children and Spouse: The estate is divided among the children and the surviving spouse.
  • Second: Parents: If there are no children, the parents inherit.
  • Third: Siblings: If there are no parents, the siblings inherit.
  • Fourth: Other Relatives: Distant relatives may inherit in the absence of closer relatives.

Note: The shares of children and spouses may differ depending on the applicable law.

Testamentary Succession

Testamentary succession occurs when a person dies leaving a valid will. A will allows you to:

  • Specify who inherits your assets
  • Appoint guardians for minor children
  • Make charitable bequests
  • Appoint an executor to manage your estate

Requirements for a Valid Will in Nepal

  • Written Form: A will must be in writing.
  • Signed by the Testator: The person making the will must sign it.
  • Witnessed: The will must be witnessed by at least one competent witness.
  • Sound Mind: The testator must be of sound mind when making the will.
  • Voluntary: The will must be made voluntarily, without coercion or undue influence.

Wills written entirely in the testator's handwriting (holograph wills) do not require witnesses.

Advantages of Having a Will

  • Control: You decide who receives your assets.
  • Avoid Disputes: A clear will reduces the risk of family disputes.
  • Protect Heirs: You can appoint guardians for minor children.
  • Make Charitable Gifts: You can leave assets to charitable organisations.
  • Simplified Process: A valid will simplifies the probate process.

Probate Process

Probate is the court-supervised process of administering an estate. The process involves:

  • Filing the Will: The will is filed with the competent court.
  • Notice: Heirs and other interested parties are notified.
  • Verification: The court verifies the validity of the will.
  • Estate Administration: The executor administers the estate.
  • Distribution: Assets are distributed to the heirs as per the will or intestate succession laws.

Common Estate Planning Mistakes

  • Dying without a will (intestate)
  • Not updating a will after major life events (marriage, divorce, birth of children)
  • Failing to appoint a reliable executor
  • Not considering tax implications
  • Leaving assets to minors without appointing a guardian

  • क्षतिपूर्ति दाबी
  • रोजगारी र श्रम विवाद

देवानी मुद्दाका चरणहरू

१. पूर्व-मुद्दा चरण

मुद्दा दायर गर्नु अघि, पक्षहरूले वार्ता वा मध्यस्थता मार्फत समाधान गर्ने प्रयास गर्नुपर्छ। यदि समाधान हुँदैन भने, वादीले मुद्दा दायर गर्न सक्छ।

२. उजुरी दायर

वादीले उचित अदालतमा उजुरी (बहसपत्र) दायर गर्छ। उजुरीमा निम्न उल्लेख हुनुपर्छ:

  • पक्षहरूको नाम र ठेगाना
  • तथ्य र कानूनी आधारको स्पष्ट विवरण
  • माग गरिएको राहत
  • सहायक कागजात र प्रमाण

३. लिखित जवाफ र बचाउ

प्रतिवादीले उजुरीको जवाफमा लिखित जवाफ पेश गर्नुपर्छ। जवाफमा प्रत्येक आरोपको जवाफ दिनुपर्छ।

४. प्रमाण र बहस

दुवै पक्षले सान्दर्भिक कागजात र जानकारी आदान-प्रदान गर्छन्। साक्षीहरूको परीक्षण र विशेषज्ञ बयान पेश गर्न सकिन्छ।

५. फैसला र डिक्री

अदालतले अन्तिम फैसला गर्छ र डिक्री जारी गर्छ। डिक्री कार्यान्वयन योग्य हुन्छ।

६. पुनरावेदन

कुनै पनि पक्ष उच्च अदालतमा पुनरावेदन गर्न सक्छ।

कानूनी सहायता

देवानी मुद्दामा विशेष कानूनी ज्ञान चाहिन्छ। कार्की एसोसिएट्सले सबै चरणमा विशेषज्ञ प्रतिनिधित्व प्रदान गर्दछ।

अस्वीकरण: यो लेख सूचनात्मक उद्देश्यका लागि मात्र हो र कानूनी सल्लाह होइन। आफ्नो विशिष्ट कानूनी मामिलाको लागि योग्य वकिलसँग परामर्श गर्नुहोस्।

२. मौन बस्ने अधिकार प्रयोग गर्नुहोस्

तपाईंले आफ्नो विरुद्ध प्रयोग हुनसक्ने प्रश्नको जवाफ दिनु पर्दैन।

३. अभियोग बुझ्नुहोस्

आफूमाथि लागेको अभियोगको स्पष्ट व्याख्या माग्नुहोस्।

४. प्रमाण सुरक्षित राख्नुहोस्

बचाउका लागि आवश्यक कागजात र प्रमाण राख्नुहोस्।

५. धरौटको लागि आवेदन दिनुहोस्

यदि पक्राउ परेमा, वकिलले धरौटको लागि आवेदन दिनेछन्।

प्रदान गरिने वस्तु वा सेवाको विस्तृत विवरण।

३. मूल्य र भुक्तानी सर्त

कुल मूल्य, भुक्तानी तालिका, र ढिलो भुक्तानीको परिणाम।

४. वितरण र कार्य तालिका

सामान वितरण वा सेवा पूरा गर्ने समय।

५. वारेन्टी र प्रतिनिधित्व

वस्तु वा सेवाले निश्चित मापदण्ड पूरा गर्ने आश्वासन।

६. दायित्वको सीमा

प्रत्येक पक्षले अर्कोबाट दाबी गर्न सक्ने क्षतिपूर्तिको सीमा।

७. क्षतिपूर्ति

एक पक्षले अर्कोलाई केही क्षतिहरूको क्षतिपूर्ति गर्ने व्यवस्था।

८. गोपनीयता

संवेदनशील जानकारीको संरक्षण।

९. समाप्ति र नवीकरण

करार समाप्त गर्ने सर्त र सूचना अवधि।

१०. विवाद समाधान

विवाद समाधान गर्ने विधि (वार्ता, मध्यस्थता, अदालत)।

११. शासकीय कानून

कुन क्षेत्राधिकारको कानून लागू हुने।

१२. अप्रत्याशित घटना

असाधारण घटनाले करार पालना गर्न नसकेमा छुट।

व्यावसायिक सहायता

१. संचालक समितिको संरचना र स्वतन्त्रता

संचालक समितिमा कार्यकारी र गैर-कार्यकारी संचालकहरूको सन्तुलन हुनुपर्छ। स्वतन्त्र संचालकहरूले वस्तुपरक दृष्टिकोण ल्याउँछन्।

२. समितिहरूको गठन

अडिट, पारिश्रमिक, र शासन समितिहरूको गठन।

३. पारदर्शिता र खुलासा

कम्पनीले वित्तीय प्रदर्शन र जोखिमको सही र समयमै खुलासा गर्नुपर्छ।

४. शेयरधनी अधिकार

शेयरधनीहरूको मतदान, जानकारी, र सभामा सहभागिताको अधिकार।

५. नैतिक आचरण

आचारसंहिता, भ्रष्टाचार विरोधी नीति, र सूचनादाता संयन्त्र।

जोखिम पहिचान, मूल्याङ्कन, र न्यूनीकरणको प्रणाली।
